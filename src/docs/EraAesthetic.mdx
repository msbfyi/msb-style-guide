# Era & Aesthetic

Brooks Brothers meets bowling shirt. Penguin meets Palm Springs.
Dapper Day. Tiki. The old-fashioned tennis boat. Nat King Cole sideman
energy.

## Primary era: 1950s American

Bowling shirts with one high-contrast print — the kind that looks like
an artist drew it, not like a photograph. Illustrated and slightly
cartoonish is good. Photorealistic is not.

## Reference brands — where to look

- **Penguin** — references the 50s directly
- **Mr. Turk** — about 10% of the line each season hits the note
- **Paul Stuart** — piping details, contrast knit work
- **Brooks Brothers, Ralph Lauren, Tommy Hilfiger** — specific pieces,
  not whole looks

## Fabrics — structure over texture

Piqué knit · ribbed jersey · poplin · camel hair · cashmere ·
small-scale herringbone · fine wale corduroy · velvet (jacket only, at
black tie)

## Finishing · formal — round over sharp

- Pocket square: cotton, flat fold straight across — not the puff, not
  sharp points
- Notch lapel on a blazer, shawl collar on a tuxedo — peak lapel is
  too aggressive
- Midnight navy tuxedo, not black
- Patent leather shoes at black tie

## Finishing · casual — bold closures

- Slip-on over lace-up: dress loafer, bit loafer, Chelsea boot
- Laces should be incidental, never the focal point
- Piping at the cuff or an interior contrast stripe
- Traditional tennis sweater with a contrast neck stripe
- Spectator shoes — strong yes

## Headwear

Pork pie — not a large brim. A patterned hat band is better still.
Worth & Worth in New York, or Goorin Bros.
